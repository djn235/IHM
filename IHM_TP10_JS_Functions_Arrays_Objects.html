EX 01
A currency conversion module
Foundational20 min


Using the fictitious rates below (value of one unit in DZD), write:




  • toDZD(amount, currency) and fromDZD(amount, currency);
  • convert(amount, from, to = "DZD"), which uses the dinar as a pivot currency and rounds to two decimals;
  • input validation: a negative or non-numeric amount, or an unknown currency, must throw an Error with an explicit message, caught by a try...catch in the tests;
  • format(amount, currency), using Intl.NumberFormat.


Test: 250 EUR to DZD, 10 000 DZD to USD, 100 GBP to EUR, -5 EUR to DZD, 50 JPY to DZD.




Check100 GBP = 100 × 175 = 17 500 DZD = 17 500 / 150 = 116.67 EUR.




EX 02
Managing a class list
Foundational20 min


Starting from the array of student names below, perform each operation and print the array or the result after each step, noting which methods mutate the original:




  1. Add "Yacine Ferhat" at the end and "Amel Bouzid" at the beginning.
  2. Remove "Karim Haddad" using indexOf and splice.
  3. Check whether "Leila Kaci" is enrolled.
  4. Produce a new array sorted alphabetically by surname (the second word), using localeCompare, without modifying the original.
  5. Display the first three of the sorted list, joined with " | ".
  6. Produce an array of initials, e.g. "A.B.".

Part B: ApplicationExercises 3 and 4


EX 03
Order analytics
Intermediate25 min


Given the orders of an online shop, compute with array methods only (no for loop):




  1. The revenue of delivered orders.
  2. The average basket (total) of delivered orders, rounded to two decimals.
  3. The list of distinct customers, alphabetically sorted.
  4. The largest order (whole object) and the customer who placed it.
  5. Whether any order is cancelled, and whether every order exceeds 5 000 DZD.
  6. The number of orders per status, as an object.






EX 04
A bank account class
Intermediate30 min


Write a class BankAccount with:


  • a constructor taking the holder's name and an initial balance (default 0);
  • deposit(amount, label) and withdraw(amount, label), which validate the amount (strictly positive number), refuse a withdrawal that would make the balance negative beyond an authorised overdraft of 20 000 DZD, record each operation in a history (date, type, amount, label, balance after) and return the account to allow chaining;
  • a getter balance;
  • statement(), which prints the history with console.table and the final balance.


Test: open an account with 50 000 DZD, deposit a salary of 60 965.50, withdraw a rent of 40 000, withdraw 95 000 for a car down payment (which must be refused), then withdraw 75 000 for equipment (accepted within the overdraft), then print the statement.




Expected balance50 000 + 60 965.50 − 40 000 = 70 965.50. Withdrawing 95 000 would give −24 034.50, beyond the −20 000 limit: refused, the balance is unchanged. Withdrawing 75 000 gives −4 034.50, within the overdraft: accepted. Final balance: −4 034.50 DZD, with four operations in the history (the refused one is not recorded).

EX 05
Multi-level sales aggregation
Advanced30 min


From the sales records below, build with reduce a nested object { region: { month: total } }, then derive:




  1. The total per region, sorted in descending order, with each region's share of the grand total in percent.
  2. For each region, its best month.
  3. The month-on-month growth rate of the whole company (October vs September, November vs October).

EX 06
Closures and function composition
Advanced30 min


Part 1. Write createIdGenerator(prefix, start = 1), which returns a function generating identifiers such as INV-2026-0001, INV-2026-0002... Each generator keeps its own counter, inaccessible from outside.


Part 2. Write small pure pricing functions (applyDiscount(rate), addVat(rate), roundTo(decimals)), each returning a function of a price, then pipe(...fns), which composes them from left to right with reduce. Build a retailPrice pipeline: 10% discount, then 19% VAT, then rounding to 2 decimals.


Part 3. Serialise an invoice containing a Date with JSON.stringify, parse it back with a reviver function so that the date becomes a Date object again, and prove it by calling a date method.




CheckretailPrice(10000): 10 000 × 0.9 = 9 000; × 1.19 = 10 710.00. Applying the VAT before the discount gives the same result, because both are multiplications, but rounding at an intermediate step would not commute: composition order matters as soon as non-linear steps are involved.
