EX 01
A simplified payslip
Foundational15 min


An employee earns a base salary of 65 000 DZD and a performance bonus of 8 000 DZD. For this exercise we use simplified rules (not the official scales): a social security deduction of 9% of the gross salary, then a withholding tax of 15% applied only to the part of the taxable amount (gross minus social security) that exceeds 30 000 DZD.


Compute and print, with template literals and two decimals: gross salary, social security, taxable amount, tax and net salary. Declare the rates as constants in UPPER_SNAKE_CASE.




Expected resultsGross 73 000; social security 6 570; taxable 66 430; tax 0.15 × 36 430 = 5 464.50; net 60 965.50 DZD.




EX 02
Cleaning form inputs
Foundational20 min


Values read from form fields are always strings. Convert each of the following raw inputs into a number, or report it as invalid. Use Number(), parseFloat(), trim() and replace() as appropriate, and test validity with Number.isNaN().




Explain why Number("") is dangerous for an amount field, and why parseFloat("12abc") may hide an input error.




PitfallsNumber("") and Number("   ") return 0, not NaN: an empty amount would silently become zero. parseFloat("12abc") returns 12, ignoring the garbage; Number() is stricter and returns NaN. For validation, prefer Number() after explicit cleaning, and reject empty strings first.

EX 03
Grades, mentions and opening hours
Intermediate25 min


Part 1. Given an array of grades out of 20, print for each grade its mention: "Very good" (≥ 16), "Good" (≥ 14), "Fairly good" (≥ 12), "Pass" (≥ 10), "Fail" (below 10). Invalid values (negative, above 20, or not numbers) must print "Invalid grade". Test with [17.5, 14, 9.75, 12, 20, -1, "abc", 10].


Part 2. With a switch, print the opening hours of a shop for a given day: Sunday to Wednesday 08:00-18:00; Thursday 08:00-13:00; Friday closed; Saturday 09:00-17:00. Group identical cases instead of repeating them. Test every day of the week with a loop over an array of day names.






EX 04
Investment tables
Intermediate25 min


Part 1. A capital of 500 000 DZD is invested at 6.5% per year with annual compounding. With a while loop, print the capital at the end of each year until it has doubled, then the number of years needed. Compare with the "rule of 72" approximation (72 / rate).


Part 2. A wholesaler grants a discount by quantity: 0% below 10 units, 5% from 10, 10% from 50, 15% from 100. With a for loop, print a price table for the quantities 1, 10, 25, 50, 75, 100 and 150 of an item at 1 200 DZD, showing quantity, discount and amount payable. Skip (with continue) any quantity whose amount would exceed a budget of 150 000 DZD, and print "over budget" instead.




CheckThe capital doubles after 12 years: 999 575.70 DZD at the end of year 11, just short of the target, and 1 064 548.12 DZD at the end of year 12. The rule of 72 gives 72 / 6.5 ≈ 11.1 years, a good approximation of the exact value ln 2 / ln 1.065 ≈ 11.0 years; since interest is credited only once a year, the target is reached at the end of year 12.

EX 05
Tiered electricity billing
Advanced25 min


An electricity supplier bills consumption by tiers (fictitious tariff for the exercise): the first 125 kWh at 2.00 DZD/kWh, the next 125 kWh (126 to 250) at 4.00 DZD/kWh, and every kWh beyond 250 at 5.50 DZD/kWh. A fixed fee of 150 DZD is added, and the whole is subject to a 9% tax, except for customers flagged as "social", who are exempt from the tax.


Write the computation without any function for the moment, inside a loop over the following customers, and print a detailed line for each: consumption per tier, energy cost, fee, tax and total.






Hand check for C-003 (320 kWh)125 × 2 + 125 × 4 + 70 × 5.5 = 250 + 500 + 385 = 1 135; + 150 = 1 285; tax 9% = 115.65; total 1 400.65 DZD.




EX 06
Verifying EAN-13 barcodes
Advanced30 min


Products sold in Algeria carry EAN-13 barcodes starting with the prefix 613. The thirteenth digit is a check digit computed from the first twelve: multiply the digits in odd positions (1st, 3rd, ...) by 1 and those in even positions by 3, add the products, and take the amount needed to reach the next multiple of 10 (0 if the sum is already a multiple of 10).


For each code of the list below, print whether it is valid, invalid (wrong check digit, with the expected digit) or malformed (not exactly 13 digits). Additionally, indicate whether a valid code was registered in Algeria. Use a loop over the characters, the remainder operator and break to stop scanning a malformed code as soon as a non-digit is found.






Worked example: 6131234567893Digits 6 1 3 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9, weights 1 3 1 3...: 6 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 2 + 9 + 4 + 15 + 6 + 21 + 8 + 27 = 107. Next multiple of 10 is 110, so the check digit is 3: the code is valid.
