01
Motion as CommunicationWhy, when and how long




In a well-designed interface, motion is never decorative alone. It serves identifiable functions:




1
Feedback
Confirms that an action was registered: a button that depresses, a field that shakes on error.


2
Continuity
Explains a change of state: a panel that slides in from the side it was summoned from.


3
Orientation
Shows where content comes from and goes to, preserving the user's mental model.


4
Attention and waiting
Signals new information, or makes a delay perceptibly shorter (loaders, skeleton screens).






InteractionRecommended duration


Micro-feedback (hover, press, toggle)100 to 200 ms


Small components (menus, tooltips, accordions)200 to 300 ms


Large surfaces (dialogs, page transitions)300 to 500 ms




Below 100 ms, a change is perceived as instantaneous; beyond roughly 500 ms, it begins to feel sluggish and obstructs the task.







02
CSS TransitionsInterpolating between two states




A transition interpolates a property smoothly when its computed value changes, whether through a pseudo-class (:hover, :focus-visible), a class added by JavaScript, or a media query. It is declared on the element in its base state.




LonghandRoleExample


transition-propertyWhich properties to animatetransform, background-color


transition-durationHow long250ms


transition-timing-functionThe acceleration curveease-out


transition-delayWaiting time before starting0s




Only interpolable properties can transition (lengths, colours, numbers, transforms); display and font-family, for example, change discretely. Avoid transition: all, which animates unintended properties and costs performance.









03
Timing Functionsease, cubic-bezier() and steps()




A timing function maps elapsed time (horizontal axis) to animation progress (vertical axis). Natural objects accelerate and decelerate; linear motion therefore appears mechanical. As a rule, ease-out suits elements entering the screen, ease-in those leaving it, and ease-in-out movements from one position to another.

Cubic Bezier editor


A cubic-bezier(x1, y1, x2, y2) curve is defined by two control points. Drag the pink and blue handles: values of y above 1 produce an overshoot (a spring-like effect). The resulting curve drives the last lane of the race above.


The horizontal coordinates must stay between 0 and 1 (time cannot flow backwards); the vertical coordinates are unrestricted.







04
Transformstranslate, rotate, scale, skew and 3D




The transform property modifies the coordinate space of an element without affecting the layout of its neighbours: the element is moved, rotated or scaled visually while its original space in the flow is preserved. The functions are composed in order, each operating in the coordinate system produced by those to its left (equivalently, they act on the element from right to left), all relative to the transform-origin. Their order therefore matters: rotate(45deg) translateX(100px) differs from translateX(100px) rotate(45deg).

The third dimensionperspective, set on the parent, defines the distance between the viewer and the plane z = 0: small values exaggerate depth. transform-style: preserve-3d lets children share the parent's 3D space, and backface-visibility: hidden hides the reverse face of a rotated element: the three ingredients of a card-flip effect.







05
Keyframe Animations@keyframes and the animation properties




Whereas a transition needs a trigger and has two states, a keyframe animation runs autonomously through any number of intermediate states defined in a @keyframes rule.






PropertyValues


animation-name / -duration / -timing-function / -delayAs for transitions


animation-iteration-countA number, or infinite


animation-directionnormal, reverse, alternate, alternate-reverse


animation-fill-modenone, forwards (keep the end state), backwards (apply the start state during the delay), both


animation-play-staterunning or paused








StaggeringA sequence of elements entering one after another reads as a coherent group. Rather than writing one rule per element, assign an index through a custom property (style="--i: 3") and compute the delay: animation-delay: calc(var(--i) * 80ms).





06
Performance and AccessibilityThe rendering pipeline and motion sensitivity




The browser renders a frame in successive stages: style, layout (geometry), paint (pixels) and composite (assembly of layers on the GPU). The cost of an animation depends on the earliest stage it invalidates; to reach 60 frames per second, each frame must be produced in about 16.7 ms.




Layout: expensivewidth, height, top, left, margin, padding, font-size. Recomputes geometry, often of the whole page.


Paint: moderatecolor, background, box-shadow, border-radius. Redraws pixels without moving anything.


Composite: cheaptransform and opacity. Handled by the compositor, usually on the GPU.




Hence the rule: animate transform and opacity. To move an element, use translate rather than left; to enlarge it, scale rather than width. will-change: transform can prepare a layer in advance, but must be used sparingly, since each layer consumes memory.






Accessibility obligations


    

  • Reduced motion: users with vestibular disorders may experience nausea or dizziness from large movements. Honour prefers-reduced-motion by removing or replacing motion with a fade (WCAG 2.3.3).
    • 

  • Pause, stop, hide: any automatic movement lasting more than five seconds must be controllable by the user (WCAG 2.2.2).
    • 

  • Flashes: nothing may flash more than three times per second (WCAG 2.3.1), as this can trigger photosensitive seizures.
    • 

  • Never convey information only through motion: the end state must remain understandable.
    • 










07
Practical ExercisesFeedback, loading, depth, choreography


EX 01
An accessible call-to-action button
Foundational


Style a "Subscribe" button with a smooth feedback: on hover and on keyboard focus, it rises by 3px, its shadow deepens and its background shifts colour, in 200ms with an ease-out curve; on press (:active), it returns down instantly. Animate only the necessary properties and provide a visible focus ring.






EX 02
Loading states: spinner and skeleton screen
Intermediate


While a product list is loading, display (a) a circular spinner built with a single element and a @keyframes rotation, with an accessible text alternative, and (b) a skeleton of three cards whose grey blocks shimmer. Both must stop animating when reduced motion is requested.






EX 03
A 3D flip bank card
Intermediate


Create a bank card (front: bank name, masked number, holder; back: magnetic strip and security code area) that flips around its vertical axis on hover and on keyboard focus, with perspective and hidden back faces. The card must be focusable and the flip must be replaced by a cross-fade under reduced motion.






EX 04
Choreographing a dashboard entrance
Advanced


Animate the appearance of six KPI tiles: each fades in and rises by 20px, with a 90ms stagger computed from a custom property index, using a single @keyframes rule and animation-fill-mode: both. Then answer: (1) why both rather than forwards? (2) why animate transform rather than margin-top? (3) what is the total duration of the sequence if each animation lasts 500ms?






Answers(1) backwards, included in both, applies the first keyframe during the delay; without it, the delayed tiles would be visible at their final position, then vanish and reappear. (2) margin-top triggers layout on every frame and shifts the neighbouring tiles; transform is handled by the compositor. (3) The last tile (index 5) starts at 5 × 90 = 450ms and ends at 450 + 500 = 950ms.
